Domain-Specific Agents

Define agents around expertise, responsibility, and permissions

Instead of concentrating every domain, tool, and permission in one giant agent, connect specialists through a coordinator where needed. Let business requirements and evaluations determine agent boundaries. A single agent or explicit workflow may be sufficient for simpler work.

Different decisions require different agents.

Profitability analysis and supply planning need different expertise, calculations, and change permissions. These differences can justify separate agents. Question types such as diagnosis, prediction, and simulation do not each require their own agent.

Align context, responsibility, and permissions

Context Boundary = Responsibility / Permission Boundary is a principle for aligning scope, not a security guarantee. Data services and tools must enforce actual permissions. A coordinator does not automatically inherit every agent’s authority.

Enterprise Knowledge Foundation

Specialist responsibilities.
Shared business definitions.

Manage ontology, knowledge graph, semantic views, policies, metrics, and business rules as a shared foundation. Sharing definitions does not mean unrestricted access or a single physical store.

Ontology
Business concepts and relationships, such as customers, products, and orders.
Knowledge Graph

Semantic View

Policies
Operating, approval, and access policies.
Metrics
Metric meanings, formulas, and scope.
Business Rules
Conditions, thresholds, precedence, and exceptions.
AI-ready data models and the knowledge layer

An AI-ready data model connects object identifiers, relationships, aggregation grain, state, history, and effective dates to source data. Available inventory needs an explicit location and time basis, with clear treatment of reservations, confirmed orders, and expected receipts.

The knowledge layer is the logical interface to business knowledge and data. Combine graph queries, retrieval, and semantic views as needed, checking permissions, sources, and versions. Link metric definitions to calculation implementations without duplicating them.

Decision / Control Loop

Observe → Decide → Act → Verify → Learn

Use validated tools for calculations and rules, and agents for interpretation and exploration. Automate pre-authorized actions and obtain approval where policy requires it. Feed outcomes and recurring exceptions into reviewed knowledge and operating criteria.
