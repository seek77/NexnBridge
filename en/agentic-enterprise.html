Agentic Enterprise

AI agents prepare the evidence.
People make the decision.

We define an Agentic Enterprise by how business rules, data, human approval, and execution results work together—not by how many agents are deployed.

Where Design Begins

Start with one decision, not an agent feature

Before choosing what to automate, we identify who decides, when the decision is made, which rules and data apply, and what must be reviewed afterward.

01 DECISION
Who makes the decision, and when?
Decision scope
Identify the final decision-maker, when the decision occurs, and the choice to be made.
02 KNOWLEDGE
Which rules and exceptions apply?
Business criteria
Identify business rules, metrics, thresholds, exceptions, and approval conditions.
03 DATA
Which data supports the decision?
Data criteria
Confirm sources, calculation rules, history, and access boundaries.
04 AGENT
What should the agent prepare?
Role and limits
Define the signals, evidence, alternatives, and open questions the agent must present.
05 OPERATION
What should be recorded afterward?
Outcomes and exceptions
Record the decision and rationale, execution results, exceptions, and the next review point.

Define the decision and who is accountable for it
before choosing the technology.

Roles & Accountability

Keep evidence preparation, decision authority, and outcome tracking separate

An agent's output is not the business decision. We separate the work of preparing evidence and options from the authority to decide and the responsibility to track what happens next.

01 AI AGENT

Prepares evidence and options

Checks the relevant business rules and data, then organizes signals, evidence, options, and known limitations.

02 DECISION OWNER

Reviews the evidence and decides

Reviews the impact of each option and any open questions, then approves an option, defers the decision, or requests further analysis.

03 OPERATING PROCESS

Tracks the decision and its result

Records who decided, which evidence was used, what happened after execution, and any exceptions.

Agents prepare evidence and options.
People retain decision authority and accountability.

Choosing a First Scope

Begin with one decision that has clear boundaries

Rather than starting enterprise-wide, use four questions to select the first scope. Treat missing information as something to investigate, not a gap to fill with assumptions.

01 RECURRENCE

Does the same question come up repeatedly?

Look for a decision that recurs regularly.

02 BUSINESS IMPACT

Does the decision change what happens next?

Confirm that the outcome leads to a different action or next step.

03 EVIDENCE

Are the current rules and data available?

Identify the business rules, metrics, and sources already used.

04 ACCOUNTABILITY

Is someone clearly responsible for review and the final decision?

Name who can approve, defer, or request further analysis.
