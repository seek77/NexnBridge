AI Engineering · Delivery Perspective

From problem framing to AI design and implementation

We draw on operating and consulting experience to define the problem to solve. For each business task, we specify the knowledge and analytical methods required, determine what AI should do, and validate the implementation against real business requirements.

Carry the problem through to implementation

A requirements list rarely captures every exception or the reasoning behind a business decision. At Nex & Bridge, consultants who frame the problem also implement and verify the solution, alongside specialist engineers.

Make business rules explicit

Understanding a sentence is different from handling a task according to company policy. LLMs can extract concepts and rules from documents. Ontologies capture business meaning; semantic models capture calculation criteria. Data constraints and execution permissions require separate checks in code.

Test whether a change is an improvement

Getting a better answer on a second attempt is not the same as improving the system. The cause may lie in knowledge, retrieval, or calculation. Before-and-after evaluation is part of the development process, so each change can be tested for its effect.

How the business works

Each workflow step retrieves applicable rules and evidence from shared knowledge. Use explicit workflows for known procedures and agent judgment where investigation must adapt. Prompts, RAG, semantic layers, and tool calls remain useful components.

Separate calculation, reasoning, and control

Calculation and rules

Use SQL, rules, solvers, and APIs for calculations and constraint checks. Validate units, reference dates, and outputs.

Interpretation and exploration

Agents interpret requests, investigate exceptions, and choose the next analysis or tool. Record assumptions and uncertainty.

Execution control

Enforce permissions, approvals, and stop conditions in code and services. Prevent duplicate changes on retries and verify outcomes.

From recurring exceptions to reviewed knowledge

Record exceptions and outcomes → Check causes and applicability → Propose a change → Owner review and approval → Version the change → Regression tests → Operational review

A verified relationship may update the ontology or knowledge graph; an approval condition may update policies or business rules. Fix missing data in the data pipeline and calculation errors in tools. Not every exception calls for a graph change.

Record the source, owner, scope, effective date, and version. Preserve individual decisions and execution history as decision memory, separate from approved enterprise knowledge.

Experience-Based Problem Framing

Test it against the work and the data.

We do not start by implementing a list of requirements. Relevant operating and project experience informs an initial view of the problem, its causes, and its business impact. We test that view against the client's decision criteria and data.

Development Methodology · Eval-Driven Development

Validate one use case at a time, then scale

We do not attempt to complete an agent in one pass and switch over all at once. We define the workflow and success criteria, test real inputs and outcomes, and improve the work plan, knowledge, data, tools, and interface through each cycle.

01 BUSINESS CONTEXT

Understand the Work

Examine the current workflow, decisions, inputs, exceptions, and expected outcomes.

02 WORK PLAN & EVALS

Define the Work Plan & Evals

Set the sequence of work, data and tools, human review points, and evaluation criteria.

03 BUILD & RUN

Build & Run

Run the work plan while implementing the agent, data structures, and business interface.

04 REVIEW & ERROR ANALYSIS

Review Results

Compare outputs with established work products and expert judgment, then classify errors and missing information.

05 CONTROLLED ROLLOUT

Apply the Validated Scope

Move validated use cases into the operating environment after they pass security and operational gates.

06 FEEDBACK & IMPROVE

Improve and Repeat

Add findings to the evaluation set, update the work plan, knowledge, data, tools, and interface, and run the cycle again.

Iteration Cycle
Business Context → Work Plan & Evals → Build & Run → Review Results → Controlled Rollout → Feedback & Improvement ↺
Shared architecture, security, and operational approval gates remain in place. The iteration unit is the business use case; platform controls apply across the program.
Adoption & Business Measures

Verify the system. Assess its use.

Technical acceptance and business impact require different evidence. We agree on a baseline and measurement conditions, then review both release readiness and practical adoption within the project scope.

Baseline and acceptance

Agree on the workflow, target measures, and comparison basis with the client. Test correctness, permissions, and failure handling before release. Processing time, manual effort, and errors are possible measures, selected for the use case.

Adoption and review

Introduce verified workflows with clear operating and exception-handling procedures. Review usage, rework, and processing time. Record the measurement period, data conditions, and external factors before interpreting changes in performance.

Evidence & Governance

Keep each result linked to its evidence, reviews, and change history

Authorship alone does not establish quality. We record who produced and reviewed the work, then maintain its evidence, validation, and approval history.

Evidence

Link key claims, business rules, and data definitions to source material and data.

Validation

Check results against representative business questions, validation cases, data consistency, and system tests.

Review & Approval

Use model reviews as input. Designated reviewers or subject-matter experts approve important business knowledge and design decisions.

Traceability & Change

Maintain relationships among requirements, designs, code, tests, knowledge, and approvals, and assess the impact of changes.
