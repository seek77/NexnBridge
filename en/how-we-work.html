Business-Led Problem Framing

Define the decision before selecting the technology

We examine the business problem, current data, decision criteria, and operating process. That gives the project a clear question, scope, and basis for validation.

Business Question

Identify the question users ask repeatedly and the decision they must make after the analysis.

Decision Context

Document the metrics, business rules, constraints, exceptions, and approvals that shape the decision.

Current State & Constraints

Assess the current workflow, systems, data and knowledge, security requirements, and operational constraints.

Success Criteria

Do not judge success by model accuracy alone. Consider time spent on the task, consistency of analysis, access to evidence, user review, and whether the result can be used and maintained in operation.

Project Knowledge

Make project knowledge reusable and reviewable

Meetings, documents, analysis, and design work should not disappear into one-time deliverables. We structure business definitions, data meaning, decision criteria, exceptions, decisions, and unresolved questions as project knowledge.

Source & Context

Record where information came from and where it applies, including source documents, data definitions, interviews, and analysis.

Working Knowledge

Structure the terms, relationships, business rules, data meaning, and assumptions identified during the project.

Decision & Assumption Log

Track design decisions, selected alternatives, assumptions, and questions that still require confirmation.

Approved Knowledge

Incorporate validated and approved content into the client's knowledge graph, data standards, and operating criteria.

Project Knowledge Sequence
Source → Analysis → Working Knowledge → Review → Decision → Approved Knowledge → Knowledge Graph · Standards
Enterprise Knowledge Engineering covers the specialized methods and service scope for structuring and governing enterprise knowledge.
AI-Native Delivery

Use AI across research, design, implementation, and validation

AI is used for more than first drafts. It supports source research, comparative analysis, requirements definition, design review, code and test creation, and result validation.

Research & Analysis

Review source material and data, compare issues and alternatives, and check important facts and figures against the original material.

Design & Build

Use requirements and project knowledge to design and implement data models, agent workflows, business interfaces, and system integrations.

Multi-Model Review

Use different models to review important analyses and candidate knowledge independently. Agreement between models does not by itself validate or confirm the result.

Reusable Skills & Guidance

Turn recurring analysis, review, and build procedures into reusable skills and guidance so teams can apply the same criteria across projects.

Evidence & Governance

Keep each result linked to its evidence, reviews, and change history

Authorship alone does not establish quality. We record who produced and reviewed the work, then maintain its evidence, validation, and approval history.

Evidence

Link key claims, business rules, and data definitions to source material and data.

Validation

Check results against representative business questions, validation cases, data consistency, and system tests.

Review & Approval

Use model reviews as input. Designated reviewers or subject-matter experts approve important business knowledge and design decisions.

Traceability & Change

Maintain relationships among requirements, designs, code, tests, knowledge, and approvals, and examine the impact of changes.
