Enterprise Knowledge Engineering · Core Service

Make business rules and expert judgment
usable by agents—and reviewable by people.

We structure concepts, relationships, business rules, exceptions, and supporting evidence from policies, manuals, data definitions, business documents, and expert knowledge. We organize the results in an Ontology and Knowledge Graph and apply validation, approval, access, versioning, and change controls. Approved knowledge can then be made available to agents and applications through services and APIs.

Why Knowledge Engineering

Finding a document is not the same as applying a business rule

RAG is useful for retrieving relevant material and supporting answer generation. Applying knowledge to analysis and decisions also requires explicit definitions, rule conditions, order of application, thresholds, exceptions, effective periods, approval status, and procedures for handling conflicting evidence.

Meaning & Context

Define business concepts, attributes, relationships, states, and events so terms can be interpreted in their business context.

Rules & Exceptions

Represent calculation criteria, decision conditions, thresholds, the order in which rules apply, exceptions, and approval procedures explicitly.

Evidence & Conflict

Record source references and effective periods, and distinguish conflicting assertions so their evidence and conditions can be compared.

Ownership & Change

Manage owners, approval status, versioning, access, and change impact to define which knowledge is approved for use.

Retrieval-focused knowledge helps people and systems find relevant information. Enterprise Knowledge Engineering adds the meaning, rules, evidence, and change controls needed when that knowledge supports analysis and decisions.

Business Rules & Governance

Make identity, conflict, approval, and change rules explicit

A graph of entities and relationships is not enough for operational use. The knowledge model also needs explicit rules for deciding what represents the same entity, which relationships are permitted, and how conflicts, approval, and change are handled.

Identity & Meaning Rules

Define unique identifiers, names, attributes, business meaning, and the scope in which each entity and relationship applies.

Merge & Conflict Rules

Define when duplicate entities can be merged and how conflicting assertions, exceptions, and effective periods are handled.

Constraint & Quality Rules

Apply rules for required attributes, permitted relationships, value ranges, completeness, and consistency.

Provenance & Approval Rules

Track sources, supporting evidence, owners, validity periods, and approval status, and define which knowledge is approved for use.

Access & Change Rules

Define access rules for people and agents, together with versioning, change, retirement, and impact-review procedures.

Business-Question Validation

Use representative business questions to check whether the model provides the required knowledge and evidence and to identify gaps and unresolved areas.

LLM-Assisted Knowledge Engineering

Use LLMs to prepare structured candidates, then validate them against sources and rules

LLMs can extract candidate concepts, relationships, and business rules from documents and data. Schemas guide the extraction; outputs from multiple models can be compared; and each candidate is checked against source documents, data definitions, rules, and representative business questions. Designated reviewers separate approved knowledge from unresolved items before publication.

Source Analysis & Extraction

Identify candidate concepts, relationships, rules, and source references in policies, manuals, data dictionaries, SQL, reports, and interview notes.

Schema-Guided Ontology

Define entity types, attributes, permitted relationships, and output structures to control what is extracted and how it is represented.

Entity Resolution & Linking

Match the same entity across different names and codes, then link documents, data, business objects, and supporting evidence.

Evidence & Multi-Model Review

Compare outputs from multiple LLMs and verify candidates against source documents and data definitions to identify omissions, unsupported inferences, and conflicts.

Rule & Quality Validation

Check identifiers, required attributes, permitted relationships, and business rules, and use representative questions to validate the structure and content.

Approval, Versioning & Change

Separate approved knowledge from unresolved items and manage approval status, versioning, change and retirement history, and impact on agents and applications.

Checking extracted knowledge — Cross-model review helps reveal omissions and differences in interpretation. The evidence remains the source material and business criteria, not agreement between models. Unresolved claims are kept separate from approved knowledge.
Methods and executable skills — A revised document should not require the extraction process to be designed again from scratch. Skills capture the procedure, input and output schemas, tools, and exception handling alongside validation code and test cases. Permissions and stopping conditions are enforced in code. Knowledge and skill changes follow the client's development and approval process, using Git or another suitable versioning system.
Knowledge Architecture

Business meaning belongs in the AI design

Even “revenue” can mean different things across departments and reports. Customer definitions, cost allocations, and exceptions also vary by business. Knowledge engineering makes those distinctions explicit and implements the criteria an AI system needs to use.

Business terms and relationships

A sold-to account and an end customer answer different questions about sales performance. An ontology defines those concepts and their relationships, mapped to source data. RDF and OWL are options where systems need a shared vocabulary or formal inference across relationships.

Missing data and invalid records

A product with no recorded cost must not appear to be highly profitable. Checks for required values, permitted relationships, and ranges identify records that need attention. RDF graphs can be validated with SHACL; tables can use SQL checks and supported database constraints.

Metric calculations and aggregation

When returns enter revenue, or how overhead is allocated, changes what a number means. A semantic model captures formulas, reporting grain, periods, and join rules for implementation in semantic views or approved SQL. Results are reconciled to reference reports or test data.

Evidence relevant to the analysis

Once a policy changes, finding a similar document is not enough. A knowledge service checks effective dates, approval status, and access before providing evidence. Keyword and vector search, SQL, and graph queries are selected to suit the material, with source and version information retained.

The boundary between analysis and action

Proposing a price change and applying it require different permissions. Code checks tool inputs, business conditions, and approvals before execution. Writes and external transmissions need duplicate-action prevention, retry rules, and audit records.

The impact of a changed definition

One revised allocation rule can affect metrics, reports, and an agent's explanation. Version and dependency records identify the knowledge, rules, skills, and query services involved. Affected analyses are retested, with release and rollback scope based on those dependencies.

Architecture fitted to the client — Existing data and search services can be used, with graph databases or OWL reasoners selected for specific traversal and inference requirements. A dedicated Knowledge Graph product is not a prerequisite. The technology configuration is determined during architecture assessment.

Design Example · Profitability Analysis

What explains a decline in profitability?

The question “Which products have become less profitable, and why?” requires more than a calculation. This design example shows how the underlying definitions shape both the knowledge model and the analysis.

Choose the right basis for comparison

Gross profit and contribution margin answer different questions. Specify the metric, comparison period, allocation rules, and treatment of one-off costs, then map those definitions to product, customer, and transaction data.

Identify gaps before calculating

Check missing costs, duplicate codes, and currency or unit mismatches. Rather than fill unresolved costs with silent assumptions, distinguish what can be analyzed from what needs further investigation.

Calculate the drivers of change

Use tested SQL and calculation modules to assess price, volume, mix, and cost effects. The LLM supports analytical sequencing and interpretation; code checks that individual contributions reconcile to the total variance.

Support review and follow-up

Keep metric and rule versions, the data cut-off, assumptions, and exceptions with the result. Provide evidence for a pricing or portfolio review, while actual changes remain subject to the relevant permissions and approvals.
