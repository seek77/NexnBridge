Profitability Context

Revenue and margin alone do not show the resources and operating complexity behind each product, customer, and channel

Products and customers with similar reported revenue and margin can place very different demands on inventory, logistics, production, sales, and service teams. The analysis needs to show both how costs are incurred and how a proposed action may affect the rest of the portfolio.

Product Economics

Analyze revenue, variable costs, contribution margin, pricing, discounts, and cost changes by product and SKU.

Cost-to-Serve

Analyze the costs of logistics, storage, small-order handling, returns, and service activities by customer, channel, and order.

Inventory & Working Capital

Review inventory turnover and aging, shelf life, inventory disposition risk, and working-capital burden alongside profitability.

Capacity & Complexity

Examine constrained production, procurement, and logistics resources and the complexity created by the SKU portfolio.

Analytics Scope

Analyze profit drivers and portfolio options across products, customers, and channels

Product & SKU Profitability

Assess revenue, cost, contribution margin, and the effects of pricing and product-mix changes by product and SKU.

Customer & Channel Profitability

Analyze profitability by customer and channel using commercial terms, service costs, and order patterns.

Cost-to-Serve Analysis

Connect the costs of order processing, storage, transport, returns, and support activities to the customers, channels, and orders served.

Product Mix & Capacity Economics

Compare how product mix and the use of constrained resources affect overall portfolio profit and operating efficiency.

Lifecycle & Inventory Exposure

Analyze product lifecycle, demand shifts, inventory aging, and disposition risk through a financial and operating lens.

Portfolio Scenario Analysis

Compare the financial and operational implications of changes in price, cost, demand, product mix, and portfolio composition.

SKU Rationalization

SKU rationalization requires more than discontinuing unprofitable items

Discontinuing a SKU based only on current profitability can cause fixed costs to be reallocated across remaining products and have unintended effects on sales by customer and channel, production efficiency, and inventory disposition. The decision needs to consider the SKU’s economics, its operating and strategic roles, and the expected effects of discontinuation.

Economic Contribution

Assess revenue, variable costs, and contribution margin, and distinguish avoidable costs from costs that would remain after an action.

Demand Health

Review demand volume, growth, variability, customer and channel concentration, and substitution options.

Inventory Exposure

Examine finished goods and raw materials, inventory aging, shelf life, and options to consume, convert, or dispose of stock.

Operational Complexity

Assess changeovers, minimum order quantities (MOQ), small orders, logistics, quality controls, and other complexity associated with the SKU.

Strategic Role

Review the SKU’s role in key customer and channel relationships, the broader product range, cross-selling, and market entry.

Discontinuation Impact

Model fixed cost reallocation, inventory and raw-material drawdown, substitution, and possible customer attrition after discontinuation.

The application compares options to retain, improve, reprice, move to another channel, scale back, or discontinue, together with their assumptions and expected impacts.

Solution Architecture

Combine the profitability model, business knowledge, scenario engine, and agent skills in one application

We do not rely on a single P&L table or treat an LLM as the analytical engine. Data and calculation logic, cost-allocation rules, business rules, and scenario models are managed separately; the agent follows the analytical workflow defined for the task.

Profitability Data Foundation

Organize revenue, cost, inventory, order, product, customer, channel, and operational activity data at the required level of detail.

Profitability Model

Implement calculation rules for contribution margin, cost-to-serve, avoidable costs, and portfolio P&L.

Business Rules & Knowledge

Structure cost allocation rules, exceptions, product and customer classifications, strategic constraints, and review criteria.

Scenario Engine

Estimate, under stated assumptions, the effects of changes in price, cost, demand, product mix, and portfolio composition, along with any resulting fixed cost reallocation.

Agent Skills

Define procedures for profitability diagnosis, driver analysis, portfolio segmentation, and scenario review as agent skills.

Decision Support Application

Present evidence, likely drivers, scenario impacts, unresolved items, and proposed actions for human review.

Data & Integration

Connect finance, sales, supply chain, and operations data using common analytical definitions

Finance & Costing

Connect revenue, standard and actual costs, cost allocations, budgets, and actuals to the profitability model.

Sales & Customer

Use data on customers, channels, contracts, pricing, discounts, promotions, and orders in the analysis.

Supply Chain & Operations

Connect data on demand, inventory, production, procurement, logistics activities, and resource usage to cost-to-serve and portfolio economics.

Planning & Execution

Support the incorporation of agreed portfolio actions into budgets and sales, procurement, and production plans, then track outcomes.
